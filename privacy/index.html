Privacy policy

Last updated: 19 October 2026

This policy explains what Dead Zone collects, why, who can see it, where it is stored and how to delete it.

Dead Zone is made by Luke McShanag-Munn, an independent developer in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia (ABN 93 790 623 704) ("we", "us"). Contact: luke.mcshanagmunn@gmail.com.

Our approach

Some small businesses are exempt from the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). Whether or not that applies to us, we handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles. We collect only what Dead Zone needs to work, we do not sell it, and we do not use it for advertising.

What stays on your iPhone

Dead Zone uses Apple's Screen Time framework (Family Controls) to block apps.

  • The apps and categories you choose are stored by iOS as tokens that Dead Zone cannot read. We never see which apps you choose or use.
  • Per-app usage times are not available to Dead Zone and are not collected.
  • Your block list, your adult website filter setting and the day you last reached your Daily Limit are stored only on your iPhone.
  • Your onboarding answers are saved on your iPhone while you set up the app.
  • A marker in your iPhone's keychain that lets Dead Zone notice when it has been deleted and installed again.
  • Dead Zone start notifications are scheduled on your iPhone. If you wrote your own words on the Why screen, the notification can include them ("You said: ..."), so they may appear on your lock screen. You can turn off a zone's start notification in its settings, or edit your words in Your goals.
  • Your Daily Limit counts time on your whole phone. iOS does the counting on your iPhone. Dead Zone saves one total for each day and when it was last updated. Which apps you used is never collected.
  • How often you want Daily Limit reminders is stored only on your iPhone.

What we collect

We collect information from you when you use the app, from your iPhone through Apple's frameworks, and from Apple when you buy Dead Zone Plus.

Account

  • Your email address, used to sign in with an email code or a password.
  • Sign-in records, such as times, IP address and device details, kept by Supabase.

Profile

  • Your display name, if you set one.
  • Your username. It is unique and lowercase (3 to 20 letters, numbers or underscores). Anyone signed in to Dead Zone can find you by typing at least 3 characters of it. You can change it in Settings. Usernames are checked against a list of blocked words.
  • Your birth year, used for the life-in-months view and to confirm you are 18 or over.
  • Your goals, the things you chose in onboarding.
  • Your Daily Limit and your iPhone's time zone.
  • The reason you wrote for using Dead Zone, if you add one.

Screen time totals

  • A daily total of your screen time. iOS tells Dead Zone when your use passes set points during the day, and Dead Zone saves the highest point reached as that day's total, and the time it was last updated. It does not say which apps were used.
  • Your streak of days under your Daily Limit.

Dead Zones and Lock-ins

  • How many apps and websites you block. Only the number, not which ones.
  • Each day a Dead Zone ran without a request to break it, used for the Leaderboard.
  • Each Dead Zone's name, start and end times and days. A Dead Zone is a recurring blocker with set times and days, an ongoing commitment.
  • Lock-ins you start or join: length, start, end and whether you finished. The apps a Lock-in blocks stay on your iPhone. A Lock-in is a one-off, unscheduled stretch off your phone.

Blocking activity only you see

These come from Screen Time. They are saved to your account for you and are never shown to your groups or friends.

  • Counts of attempts to open a blocked app, and whether a Dead Zone or the Daily Limit blocked them. Not which app.
  • When Screen Time access is turned off or back on.

Activity your group can see

These come from what you do in Dead Zone, not from Screen Time.

  • When you delete a Dead Zone, switch blocking off or raise your Daily Limit. Not the new limit.
  • Slaps and hugs you send and receive.
  • When you end a Dead Zone early with a code.
  • When Dead Zone finds it was deleted and installed again.

Blocked people and reports

  • People you block. The list of who you blocked is stored on our server. It is removed when you unblock someone or delete your account. Blocking someone also removes any friendship or pending request between you and hides each of you from the other's search.
  • Reports you send. A report includes your display name and username and the other person's display name and username, the group name, the reason you chose, an optional note of up to 500 characters, and the text you reported.

Each report is emailed to us at the contact address above. The email is sent through Gmail now, and we plan to send it through Resend.

Requests to change your settings

When you ask to turn off the adult website filter, unblock apps, delete or change a Dead Zone, raise your Daily Limit, or end a running Dead Zone or Lock-in early: what you asked for, the groupmate you asked (if any), the code they share with you, the sentence you typed (which includes your own phone time, such as "I spent 3h 10m on my phone yesterday"), an optional reason of up to 140 characters, and when it was approved, used or expired.

Groups

  • Groups you create or join (up to 10), their invite codes, and when you joined. Each group you are in sees the same things about you, as described below.
  • When someone leaves a group, a short notice with their display name, kept for the people still in it.

Friends

  • Friends. When you send, accept or decline a friend request, we store who asked whom, its status and when. Only the two of you can see it. Removing a friend or blocking someone deletes the friendship.
  • Finding people. Search shows only a username, display name and whether you are already friends. It never shows email, stats or groups. People you have blocked, or who have blocked you, do not appear. To prevent misuse, searches and friend requests are limited per account.
  • A person's profile. Someone who finds you can see your display name, username and the month you joined. Your Leaderboard stats are shown only to groupmates.
  • Group invites. A friend can invite you to one of their groups from your profile. We store the group, who invited whom and whether you joined or dismissed it. The invite sends you that group's name and code.
  • You get a notification when someone sends you a friend request, accepts yours or invites you to a group.

Notifications

  • If you allow notifications, a push token for your iPhone. It is removed when you sign out.

Purchases

  • Your Dead Zone account ID is linked to our subscription provider so the app knows whether you have Dead Zone Plus. If you subscribe or buy lifetime, Apple handles payment and our subscription provider receives your purchase records. We never receive your card details.

Dead Zone does not include analytics or advertising tools, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Why we use it

  • To run your account and sign you in.
  • To block apps, apply your Daily Limit and show your numbers.
  • To run friends, groups, Lock-ins, slaps, hugs and notifications.
  • To manage subscriptions and purchases.
  • To review reports and keep Dead Zone safe and secure.
  • To fix problems and answer your questions.

We use your information only for these purposes, or as the law requires or allows. We do not send marketing emails.

Who can see your data

Your group. Nothing from Screen Time is ever shared with your group or your friends. Your daily screen time total, your streak, whether you are under or over your Daily Limit, when your total was last updated, your attempts to open blocked apps and whether Screen Time access is on are seen only by you. Screen Time information is used to block apps and to show you your own figures. It is never used for advertising and we do not sell it or give it to data brokers. This is enforced by our servers, not only hidden in the app.

What your group sees. Your display name and username, your Lock-ins, the slaps and hugs you send and receive, the names, times and days of your Dead Zones, your current status: in a Dead Zone (its name and end time), in a Lock-in, or free, when you last ended a Dead Zone or Lock-in early with a code (your last slip), when you delete a Dead Zone, switch blocking off or raise your Daily Limit, and your place on the Leaderboard. The Leaderboard counts only requests to end or weaken a block, blocks ended with a friend's code, Lock-ins and Dead Zone days kept. These do not use Screen Time information. Dead Zone never collects or shows your location. Status comes only from the times you set. If you are in more than one group, each group sees the same.

Unlock requests. A groupmate you ask to approve a change sees what you asked for, such as unblocking apps or raising your Daily Limit, and any short reason you wrote with the request. When you ask for a code, the app shows you what you said on the Why screen. It is sent to the groupmate you ask only if you turn on "Show this to them" for that request, and then becomes part of the request's reason (up to 140 characters). A code can come from anyone you share a group with. Groupmates never see which apps you use, your email address, your birth year, your goals or your push token.

Leaving a group. You can leave your group at any time on the Community tab without deleting your account. Your membership is removed straight away, the group stops seeing your activity and you stop seeing theirs, and the people still in the group are told you left. Your Dead Zones and Daily Limit stay on. A change you are waiting to unlock moves to typing a sentence instead of a code. If you created the group, it passes to the longest-standing member. If you were the last member, the group is deleted. Leaving one group does not change what your other groups see.

Reports and blocks. Only we read reports. The person you report and your group cannot see them. Your list of blocked people is visible only to you.

Notifications to your group. Dead Zone sends your group notifications about some of the activity above, such as starting a Lock-in, ending a Dead Zone early with a code or deleting a Dead Zone. Notifications never include your screen time, blocked app attempts or Screen Time access.

Service providers. These providers help run Dead Zone. They handle data for us to provide their service:

  • Supabase: our database, sign-in and server functions. Our database is hosted in Tokyo, Japan.
  • Expo: delivers notifications and app updates. It receives your push token and basic device details such as the app version.
  • Apple: delivers notifications, takes payments and provides the Screen Time framework.
  • RevenueCat: tracks subscription and purchase status. It receives your Dead Zone account ID, purchase records and basic device details.
  • Our email provider, currently Google (Gmail): sends sign-in codes to your email address, and the emails we receive about reports. We plan to move to Resend, which would then send both. We will update this page when we do.

Others. We share information only if the law requires it, for example in response to a valid legal request, or to protect someone's safety.

Data stored outside Australia

Our database is in Japan. Our providers are based in the United States and may also process data in other countries where they or their sub-processors operate. We choose established providers that are bound by their terms to keep data secure and use it only to provide their service to us. Your information is not stored in Australia.

Security

  • Data is sent over encrypted connections.
  • Rules in our database let other users see only what is described above. Server keys are never in the app.
  • At Dead Zone, only Luke McShanag-Munn has administrative access.

No system is perfectly secure. If a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm, we will tell you as soon as we can and notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner where required.

How long we keep it

We keep your data while you have an account, and delete it when you delete your account. The exception is reports, described below.

  • Our database. Deleting your account removes your data from our live database straight away, except reports. Our current Supabase plan does not keep daily backups or point-in-time recovery copies of the database, so no backup copies of your data are kept that we could restore. Supabase may keep short-lived operational logs and its own infrastructure recovery copies, which it deletes in the normal course. If we move to a plan with backups, we will update this page with how long backups are kept.
  • Reports. Reports are kept for 12 months, even if the account is deleted, for safety. After that they are deleted. A report keeps the display names as they were at the time, the group name, the reason, the note and the reported text, but is no longer linked to an account once the account is deleted. We also delete the emails we received about a report after 12 months.
  • Sign-in emails. Because sign-in codes are currently sent from our Gmail account, a copy of each email (your address and a one-time code) is kept in that account's sent mail. We delete these copies at least once a month.
  • Purchase records. Apple and RevenueCat keep purchase records under their own policies and legal obligations, such as tax law.
  • Emails you send us. We keep your messages for as long as we need to deal with your request, then delete them.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in Settings at any time. Deletion is immediate. It removes your account, profile, screen time totals, streak, Dead Zones, Lock-ins, group memberships, username, friendships, friend requests, group invites, blocked-attempt counts, protection changes, requests, slaps, hugs and your list of blocked people. Reports you sent or that name you are kept, as described above. If you created a group that still has other members, the group passes to the longest-standing member. If you were the only member, the group is deleted. Deleting your account also removes every block on your iPhone.

Deleting your account does not cancel a Dead Zone Plus subscription. Cancel it in your iPhone's Settings: tap your name, then Subscriptions.

Your choices

  • Leave a group on the Community tab. Your account and your blocks stay.
  • Turn off notifications in your iPhone's Settings.
  • Turn off Screen Time access in your iPhone's Settings. Dead Zone can't block apps without it. Your group is not told.
  • Delete your account in Settings.
